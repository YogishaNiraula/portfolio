# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Dual audience — recruiters and hiring managers assessing Yogisha for frontend roles, and potential freelance clients or collaborators evaluating her for project work. Both arrive to judge credibility, taste, and technical competence through the work shown. The site must convert either intent into a real-world contact.

## Product Purpose

Personal portfolio for Yogisha Niraula, a self-taught frontend developer from Nepal expanding into backend. The site showcases shipped projects, communicates skill range, conveys personality, and funnels visitors toward email, GitHub, or LinkedIn. Success means a visitor leaves with a clear sense of capability and a reason to reach out.

## Brand Personality

Polished, elegant, creative. The site should feel artfully crafted rather than templated. Confidence through restraint — no shouting, no gimmicks, no over-explaining. The design itself is part of the portfolio; it demonstrates taste and attention to detail before a single project card is read.

## Anti-references

- Dark neon cyberpunk aesthetics (glow-heavy, futuristic, aggressive)
- Cookie-cutter devfolio templates (icon grids, gradient heroes, identical card layouts)
- Tech-bro maximalism (overly busy, competitive energy, loud CTA patterns)
- Unstyled brutalism or raw-HTML minimalism

## Design Principles

1. **Taste over volume.** Every element earns its place. A single well-executed detail outweighs a dozen safe defaults.
2. **The site is the first project.** The portfolio itself demonstrates craft — typography, spacing, motion, color — before any case study loads.
3. **Invite, don't sell.** The tone is open and warm, not transactional. Contact feels like a natural next step, not a conversion funnel.
4. **Nepali presence without cliché.** The cultural origin should register subtly (palette, type rhythm, restraint) without leaning on flag colors, mandalas, or overt symbolism.
5. **Elegance through precision.** Not ornamentation. Tight spacing, deliberate contrast, purposeful whitespace.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Basic consideration. Reasonable effort toward readable contrast, keyboard navigation, and focus management. No formal WCAG compliance target, but the site should not exclude anyone from its primary function.
